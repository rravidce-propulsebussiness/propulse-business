Construction FAQs

Structure, materials, concrete, waterproofing and quality

Why is a lintel necessary above doors and windows?

A lintel bridges the opening and transfers masonry or wall load to the supports on both sides instead of allowing that load to bear directly on the door or window frame. The lintel size, reinforcement and bearing should follow the structural or project drawings rather than a generic rule.

What causes honeycombing in concrete?

Honeycombing is commonly associated with poor compaction, congested reinforcement, leaking formwork, unsuitable workability, segregation or concrete that cannot properly flow around bars and corners. Prevention starts with a workable approved mix, tight formwork, planned placement and correct vibration. Serious honeycombing should be assessed before repair because depth and location matter.

Why do cracks form in a new house?

Cracks can come from several different mechanisms: drying or thermal shrinkage, movement between RCC and masonry, settlement, poor curing, plaster workmanship, moisture changes or structural movement. The pattern, width, direction and whether the crack is growing matter. Wide, diagonal, recurring or structural-looking cracks should be reviewed by a qualified professional.

Which steel is stronger for house construction: Fe500 or Fe500D?

Both designations indicate minimum yield-strength classes under the reinforcement standard, while the D designation is associated with enhanced ductility requirements. “Stronger” alone does not decide what should be used. The structural engineer should specify the grade, bar diameter and detailing for the building. BIS lists IS 1786:2008 for high-strength deformed reinforcement bars.

Which TMT steel should I use for my house?

Use the grade and bar sizes shown in the approved structural design, from a traceable source that conforms to the specified standard. Do not substitute a different grade simply because it is marketed as premium or stronger. Verify markings, invoices and test documentation where the project quality plan requires it.

What is the best cement for house construction?

There is no single cement type that is best for every application. Cement selection depends on the approved concrete mix, exposure conditions, structural specification and intended work. OPC, PPC and PSC have different characteristics and relevant BIS standards. Keep cement dry, traceable and within the project specification.

OPC or PPC: which is better for a house?

Neither is universally better. The appropriate cement depends on the concrete mix, exposure, required early strength, heat development, curing and project specification. Use the cement type approved by the structural engineer or concrete mix designer rather than switching types based only on price or brand.

M-sand or river sand: which is better for construction?

Quality and grading matter more than the label. Properly manufactured and controlled M-sand can be suitable for concrete, while natural sand also needs acceptable grading and cleanliness. BIS lists IS 383:2016 for coarse and fine aggregates for concrete. Concrete, masonry and plaster may require different aggregate characteristics.

Red brick or AAC block: which is better for a house?

The better option depends on structural assumptions, wall thickness, local workmanship, services, plaster system, moisture detailing, fixing requirements and budget. AAC is generally lighter, while conventional clay masonry may be familiar to more local crews. Compare the complete wall system rather than only the unit price.

Why is curing necessary after concreting?

Curing helps concrete retain suitable moisture and temperature conditions for cement hydration and strength development. Poor early curing can increase surface drying and cracking risk. The curing method and duration should follow the concrete specification, cement type, member and weather conditions rather than one universal online number.

How many days should a slab be cured?

There is no single duration that is correct for every mix, cement type and weather condition. Follow the project specification and engineer’s instructions for the actual concrete used. The important point is to avoid damaging early moisture loss and to maintain the specified curing continuously.

What happens if too much water is added to concrete at site?

Uncontrolled water addition can change the designed water-cement ratio and may reduce strength, increase permeability, segregation and shrinkage risk. Workability should be managed through the approved mix and admixture process, not by casually adding water at site.

Why are cover blocks important in RCC?

Cover blocks help maintain the specified distance between reinforcement and the concrete surface. Correct cover supports durability, fire resistance and proper reinforcement position. Cover requirements depend on the member, exposure and design; random stone pieces or incorrect cover can move bars away from their intended position.

Why is vibration required when concrete is poured?

Proper vibration helps compact fresh concrete, remove trapped air and fill spaces around reinforcement and formwork. Too little vibration can leave voids or honeycombing, while excessive or poorly controlled vibration can contribute to segregation. Use trained placement and compaction practices.

What is a cold joint in concrete?

A cold joint can occur when fresh concrete is placed against concrete that has already begun to set, so the two placements do not behave like one continuous fresh pour. Construction joints should be planned and treated according to the structural and project specification rather than occurring accidentally from delays.

What is segregation in concrete and why is it a problem?

Segregation is separation of concrete constituents so the mix is no longer uniform. It can result from unsuitable workability, excessive handling, free fall, over-vibration or poor mix control. Segregated concrete can produce weak, porous or uneven zones and should not simply be covered by finishing work.

What is bleeding in concrete?

Bleeding is upward movement of water toward the surface of fresh concrete as solids settle. Some bleeding can occur naturally, but excessive bleeding can affect finishing and surface quality. Avoid finishing while bleed water remains and follow the approved concrete and placement process.

Why is a soil test important before house construction?

Foundation design depends on actual ground conditions, building loads, groundwater and settlement behaviour. Soil can vary significantly even between nearby plots. A neighbour’s footing size is not a reliable substitute for site investigation and structural design.

Can I copy the foundation design from my neighbour’s house?

No. Even nearby plots can have different fill, soil layers, groundwater or rock depth, and the buildings may have different loads and geometry. Foundation type and size should come from site-specific geotechnical information and structural design.

Why is a plinth beam provided?

A plinth beam can tie structural elements at plinth level and provide support or load distribution for masonry depending on the design. Its exact role, size and reinforcement depend on the structural system. It should not be added, omitted or resized from a generic detail.

What is DPC and why is it used?

A damp-proof course is intended to reduce moisture rising through walls or floors by capillary action. Its location and material system should be coordinated with the wall, floor and waterproofing details. DPC cannot compensate for leaking plumbing, poor site drainage or failed external waterproofing.

Why does dampness appear on new walls?

Possible causes include plumbing leaks, rain penetration, failed waterproofing, rising damp, condensation, wet plaster or trapped construction moisture. Painting over dampness without identifying the moisture source often leads to recurring stains, efflorescence or peeling.

Why is waterproofing important before tiles are fixed?

Waterproofing is easier to inspect and test before it is hidden by screed, tiles or finishes. Toilets, balconies, terraces and penetrations need correct substrate preparation, detailing, slopes and testing. A decorative tile surface itself is not the waterproofing system.

Why are slopes important on terraces and balconies?

Adequate falls help water reach drains instead of ponding. Waterproofing should not be expected to compensate for poor drainage geometry. Check outlet locations, thresholds, drain levels and final finish levels as one coordinated detail.

Why should reinforcement be inspected before slab concreting?

After concrete is poured, bar size, spacing, laps, anchorage, extra reinforcement, cover and embedded services are hidden. A qualified pre-pour inspection against the latest approved structural drawings is therefore an important quality-control step.

Can electrical conduits be placed inside an RCC slab?

Embedded conduits and sleeves may be part of a coordinated design, but their size, spacing and location should be planned so they do not interfere with reinforcement, slab depth or structural performance. Do not cut or displace reinforcement to make room for unplanned services.

Why should plumbing pipes be tested before closing walls and floors?

Pressure and leakage testing before concealment makes defects easier to find and repair. Once tiles, ceilings or boxing are complete, the same leak can require destructive access and damage finishes. Keep valves, cleanouts and service routes maintainable.

Ready-mix concrete or site-mix concrete: which is better?

Either can produce acceptable concrete when properly designed, batched, transported, placed, compacted and cured. Ready-mix offers controlled batching advantages, while small site mixes may be used for appropriate work under supervision. Choose based on structural specification, logistics and quality control rather than a universal ranking.

What should I check before an RCC slab pour?

Confirm the latest drawings, reinforcement inspection, cover blocks and chairs, formwork stability and levels, openings and sleeves, cleanliness, concrete supply plan, access, manpower, testing arrangements and curing preparation. The engineer or responsible quality person should approve the pour.

Which material is best for home construction?

There is no single “best material” list that fits every house. Material selection should consider structural design, climate and exposure, availability, workmanship, maintenance, budget and compatibility between systems. Define performance and approved grades first, then compare compliant brands and suppliers.

Why do cracks often appear where a wall meets a beam or column?

RCC and masonry can move, shrink and respond to temperature or moisture differently. Junctions can therefore become crack-prone, especially when detailing or plaster preparation is weak. Project drawings may call for mesh or other junction treatment; the correct detail depends on the wall and structural system.

Why do cracks form around doors and windows?

Openings create changes in wall geometry and stress concentration. Poor lintel/support detailing, shrinkage, movement at RCC-masonry junctions, weak plaster preparation or settlement can all contribute. Repeated diagonal or widening cracks should be professionally assessed.

What is a slump test and why is it done?

A slump test is a field check of fresh-concrete consistency or workability. It helps verify whether delivered concrete is reasonably consistent with the specified range, but it does not by itself prove concrete strength or overall quality.

Why are concrete cubes tested?

Concrete strength specimens are commonly tested to verify compressive-strength performance of sampled concrete at specified ages. Sampling, curing and testing procedures need to follow the project quality plan and applicable standards. Cube results are one part of quality control, not the only check.

What is lap length in reinforcement?

Lap splices transfer force from one reinforcing bar to another by overlapping them over a designed length. Required lap length depends on bar diameter, concrete and steel properties, stress condition, location and detailing rules. Use structural drawings; do not apply one universal multiple from social media.

Why are stirrups used in beams and columns?

Stirrups or ties contribute to shear resistance, confinement and holding longitudinal bars in position depending on the structural member. Their diameter, spacing and anchorage are structural-design items and should match approved drawings.

Can reinforcement bars be bent and straightened repeatedly?

Repeated or uncontrolled bending can damage reinforcement and reduce reliability, especially at tight bends or previously strained locations. Bar bending should follow approved schedules and standards. Do not rework critical reinforcement casually without engineer approval.

When can shuttering or formwork be removed?

Formwork removal depends on member type, span, concrete strength development, temperature, loading and the approved construction procedure. Do not use one universal number of days for every slab or beam. Follow the structural engineer and project specification.

Why is masonry curing important?

Curing can help cement-based mortar develop properly and reduce rapid moisture loss. The appropriate method and duration depend on masonry units, mortar and weather. Follow the project specification rather than assuming concrete and masonry require identical curing practice.

Why is plaster curing important?

Cement plaster can shrink or crack if it loses moisture too quickly or is poorly mixed/applied. Proper substrate preparation, mix consistency, thickness control and curing all influence finish quality. Curing should follow the plaster system and site conditions.

Why does plaster sometimes sound hollow when tapped?

A hollow sound can indicate loss of bond between plaster and the substrate, although tap testing alone cannot identify every defect. Causes can include poor surface preparation, dust, very smooth concrete, incorrect bonding treatment, improper mix or workmanship.

What causes white powder or salt marks on walls?

Efflorescence occurs when moisture dissolves soluble salts and carries them to a surface where they crystallize as water evaporates. Treating only the visible salt without resolving the moisture path can lead to recurrence.

Why do floor or wall tiles crack in a new house?

Tile cracks can result from substrate movement, voids, poor adhesive coverage, structural or screed cracks, thermal movement, impact or inappropriate joint detailing. The crack pattern and whether the substrate is also cracked should be checked before simply replacing the tile.

Why do tiles sound hollow after installation?

Hollow sound can come from incomplete adhesive or mortar contact, debonding or local voids. Not every hollow area fails immediately, but widespread hollow tiles can indicate installation-quality issues. Evaluate location, movement and actual bond before deciding on repair.

Why are expansion or movement joints needed in buildings?

Long building elements and finishes move with temperature, shrinkage and other effects. Planned movement joints allow movement to occur at controlled locations instead of forcing random cracking. Joint location and detailing are design decisions, not a decorative choice.

Should anti-termite treatment be done before construction?

Where termite risk and project specifications call for it, pre-construction termite-control measures can form part of the building protection strategy. Treatment should use legally permitted products and trained applicators, with attention to soil, foundations, service entries and later disturbance of treated zones.

Why does a terrace become very hot and what can reduce heat gain?

Roofs receive strong solar radiation and can transfer heat into rooms below. Reflective finishes, insulation, ventilated roof systems, shading and appropriate roof build-ups can reduce heat gain. The right solution depends on waterproofing, roof use, climate and construction details.

Can waterproofing chemical be mixed into concrete and solve all leakage problems?

No single admixture can compensate for cracks, poor joints, bad slopes, leaking penetrations or failed detailing. Integral waterproofing products may be one part of a specified system, but roofs, toilets, basements and joints still need coordinated design, workmanship and testing.

Why should construction materials be approved before purchase?

Approving material type, grade, brand range or performance criteria before purchase reduces substitution disputes and helps ensure compatibility with drawings and specifications. Keep approvals, invoices and test documents for critical structural and waterproofing materials.

What should I check when cement bags arrive at site?

Check the specified cement type, manufacturer/source, bag condition, markings, obvious moisture or lumping, storage arrangement and purchase records. Cement should be stored dry and protected from ground and wall moisture.

Interiors FAQs

Kitchen, wardrobes, finishes, electrical and space planning

Plywood, MDF or HDHMR: which is best for wardrobes?

The right substrate depends on moisture exposure, finish, machining, hardware loads, screw holding, budget and fabrication method. Plywood, MDF and HDHMR each have suitable applications. Ask for the exact substrate type, grade, thickness, finish and edge treatment in the quotation instead of accepting a generic “premium board” description.

What is BWP plywood and where is it useful?

BWP is commonly used as a moisture-resistant plywood category for applications where higher water resistance is desired. Suitability still depends on the exact product standard, exposure, edge sealing, fabrication and installation. Persistent building dampness should be fixed rather than relying on plywood alone.

Laminate, acrylic or PU finish: which is better for kitchen shutters?

Laminate is durable and widely available, acrylic can provide a high-gloss manufactured surface, and PU is a painted finish with broad colour and finish flexibility. Cost, scratch visibility, edge treatment, repairability and fabrication quality differ. Choose by use and desired finish rather than a universal ranking.

Modular kitchen or carpenter-made kitchen: which should I choose?

Modular systems can offer factory-controlled fabrication, repeatable modules and hardware integration, while skilled site carpentry can offer flexibility for unusual conditions. Compare drawings, substrate, hardware, finishes, edge treatment, installation process, warranty and service—not only the label “modular” or “carpenter.”

What should be finalized before ordering a modular kitchen?

Finalize appliance models or dimensions, sink and hob positions, plumbing and electrical points, cabinet layout, storage accessories, countertop, backsplash, shutter finish and hardware. Late appliance changes can force cabinet and service revisions.

How should I compare 2BHK interior quotations?

Give every company the same room-by-room scope. Compare actual cabinet dimensions, substrate and thickness, finish, hardware, internal accessories, electrical/civil work, false ceiling, painting, loose furniture, exclusions, taxes, timeline and warranty. Carpet area alone does not define cabinetry cost.

Why does interior cost vary so much for the same 2BHK?

Two 2BHK homes can have very different wardrobe lengths, kitchen layouts, ceiling work, finishes, hardware, civil changes and loose furniture. Interior cost is driven by the detailed scope and material level, not only the number of bedrooms.

What is the difference between laminate and veneer?

Laminate is a manufactured decorative surface with consistent patterns and practical maintenance. Veneer is a thin layer of natural wood, so grain and colour vary and it usually needs a finishing system. Compare appearance, maintenance, repairability, substrate and budget.

Granite or quartz: which is better for a kitchen countertop?

Both can work well when correctly selected and installed. Natural granite varies by slab and may need sealing depending on stone, while engineered quartz offers more controlled appearance but has manufacturer-specific heat and use limitations. Compare thickness, support, joints, edge details, sink cutouts and maintenance.

Why is hardware important in modular interiors?

Hinges, drawer channels, lift-up systems and accessories are moving components used repeatedly for years. Hardware grade, load rating, installation accuracy, adjustment and serviceability can affect everyday experience as much as the visible finish.

Is a false ceiling necessary for every room?

No. False ceilings can help integrate lighting, conceal services, create levels or support a design concept, but they also reduce clear height and add cost and maintenance considerations. Use them where they solve a functional or aesthetic need rather than by default.

What should I plan before making a false ceiling?

Coordinate ceiling levels with AC ducts, sprinklers where applicable, fans, curtain pockets, lighting, access panels and wardrobe heights. Confirm slab leakage or dampness is resolved before concealing the ceiling.

How many electrical points should a bedroom have?

There is no universal number. Plan around furniture layout and actual loads: bedside charging, lighting, fan, AC, TV, study/work needs, housekeeping and future flexibility. Circuit design and protection should be handled by a qualified electrical professional.

Why should electrical planning happen before interior work?

Wardrobes, TV units, kitchen cabinets, false ceilings and furniture can block or dictate switch and socket positions. Early coordination reduces visible extension wiring, rework and cutting of finished surfaces.

Can interiors start if there is dampness on the wall?

It is better to identify and correct the moisture source first. Covering an actively damp wall with cabinetry or decorative panels can trap moisture, damage finishes and hide a continuing leak. Verify plumbing, external seepage, waterproofing and wall dryness before closing the surface.

Putty, primer and paint: what is the correct sequence?

The exact system should follow the selected manufacturer’s instructions and substrate condition. In general, the wall must first be sound, dry and repaired; compatible preparation, putty/primer and finish coats then follow the specified paint system with proper drying intervals.

How can I make a small apartment feel larger?

Prioritize circulation, correctly scaled furniture, continuous visual lines, useful vertical storage, lighter visual mass, good lighting and fewer unnecessary partitions. Storage planning usually creates more practical value than adding decorative elements to every wall.

What should be included in an interior handover checklist?

Check cabinet alignment and operation, hardware adjustment, finish defects, edge sealing, glass/mirror safety, electrical points and lighting, plumbing around sinks, paint touch-ups, accessories, keys, warranties and the final approved scope. Record snags before final closure.

Marine plywood and BWP plywood: are they the same?

“Marine plywood” is often used casually in the market, while BWP refers to a water-resistance performance category. Do not rely on marketing names alone. Check the actual product standard, grade, manufacturer documentation, thickness and intended application.

What plywood thickness should be used for wardrobes?

There is no single thickness that is right for every wardrobe component. Side panels, shelves, shutters, backs and lofts may use different substrates and thicknesses based on span, load, hardware and design. Ask for a component-wise material schedule in the quotation.

Why do wardrobe shutters bend or warp?

Warping can be influenced by substrate choice, very tall or wide shutter dimensions, uneven finishing, moisture, heat, poor storage, hinge quantity/position or fabrication quality. Large shutters should be designed with suitable material, thickness and hardware rather than treated like small doors.

What is soft-close hardware and is it worth it?

Soft-close hinges and drawer systems damp the final part of closing, reducing slamming and improving feel. Value depends on hardware quality, load rating, installation and service support. A low-quality soft-close fitting is not automatically better than a good standard fitting.

Gypsum ceiling or POP ceiling: which is better?

Gypsum-board systems can offer fast, controlled dry construction, while POP is a wet-applied material useful for certain profiles and detailing. Selection depends on design, moisture conditions, workmanship, access, repairability and local execution quality.

Why do false ceilings develop cracks?

Cracks can occur at board joints, perimeter junctions, movement points or poorly supported framing, and can be influenced by moisture, structural movement or workmanship. Correct framing, joint treatment, movement detailing and dry conditions are important.

Wallpaper or paint: which is better for bedroom walls?

Paint is versatile and relatively easy to refresh, while wallpaper can provide texture and pattern but needs a sound, dry substrate and careful joint/detailing work. In damp-prone walls, fix the moisture problem before choosing either finish.

What is the best colour temperature for home lighting?

There is no single best colour temperature for every room. Warmer light is often used for relaxed living and bedrooms, while neutral or cooler light may suit task areas. More important is a coordinated lighting plan using brightness, glare control, colour rendering and layered light.

Why should kitchen chimney ducting be planned early?

Duct route, outlet location, cabinet height, chimney size and false-ceiling conditions can affect kitchen design. Planning the duct before cabinets are made avoids awkward bends, visible pipes and later cutting through finished work.

Ducted or recirculating kitchen chimney: which is better?

A ducted chimney exhausts air outside and is generally better at removing heat, moisture and cooking contaminants when a proper duct route is available. Recirculating units filter and return air indoors and may suit locations where external ducting is impractical.

Should wardrobes have ventilation?

Closed storage in humid or damp conditions can develop odour, moisture and fungal problems. Good room ventilation, dry walls and appropriate wardrobe detailing matter more than decorative vents alone. Do not install wardrobes against an unresolved damp wall.

What is the correct sequence for interior work after civil construction?

A typical sequence coordinates waterproofing and wet work first, then ceilings/services, wall preparation, fixed carpentry, countertop/glass work, painting/touch-ups, electrical fixtures and final cleaning. The exact sequence changes by project, but avoiding repeated rework between trades is the goal.

Why should appliances be selected before kitchen design is frozen?

Built-in and freestanding appliance dimensions affect cabinet widths, ventilation gaps, electrical load, plumbing and countertop cut-outs. Choosing appliances late can force expensive changes to finished cabinets and services.

What should I check in a wardrobe quotation?

Check actual width and height, internal divisions, substrate and thickness, back panel, loft, shutter finish, edge treatment, hinges, drawers, handles, accessories, installation, taxes and warranty. A rate per square foot alone can hide major scope differences.

Why do laminate edges peel or chip?

Edge failure can result from poor edge-banding or adhesive, moisture exposure, impact, weak substrate edges or fabrication quality. Good edge preparation, compatible adhesive/banding and careful installation reduce the risk.

Is wooden flooring suitable for every room?

No. Flooring selection should consider moisture exposure, subfloor flatness, maintenance, pets, sunlight, acoustic needs and manufacturer limitations. Wet areas and damp-prone rooms need particularly careful product selection and detailing.

How can I reduce interior cost without making the home look cheap?

Prioritize storage and high-use zones, simplify decorative elements, standardize modules, use durable mid-range finishes, limit unnecessary false ceilings and feature walls, and spend more on hardware or surfaces that receive heavy daily use.

Real Estate FAQs

RERA, documents, buying, resale and property checks

What is RERA and why should a homebuyer check it?

RERA is the real-estate regulatory framework intended to improve transparency, accountability and disclosure in covered projects. For Telangana projects, buyers can verify registration and disclosed project information through the Telangana RERA authority. Whether a particular project requires registration depends on the Act, state rules and project facts.

What is carpet area?

Under the RERA framework, carpet area is the net usable floor area of an apartment, excluding external walls, service shafts, exclusive balcony or verandah area and exclusive open terrace area, while including internal partition walls. Compare homes using clearly disclosed carpet area rather than only marketing terms such as super built-up area.

What is the difference between carpet area, built-up area and super built-up area?

Carpet area focuses on net usable internal floor area as defined under RERA. Built-up area commonly adds wall areas and may include balconies depending on usage, while super built-up or saleable area may also allocate common areas. Because terminology can vary, ask for the exact area statement and RERA disclosures.

What should I verify before paying a token amount for a property?

Before committing money, verify the seller or promoter identity, property details, title and approvals with qualified professionals, RERA registration where applicable, payment terms, refund/cancellation terms and what document the token payment represents. Avoid relying only on verbal assurances.

What is an occupancy certificate and why is it important?

An occupancy certificate generally indicates that the completed building has been assessed by the competent authority for occupancy under applicable approvals and rules. Requirements and terminology vary by authority and project. Buyers should verify the project’s completion/occupancy documentation with the relevant authority and their legal adviser.

What is a completion certificate?

A completion certificate generally relates to completion of construction in accordance with sanctioned plans and applicable requirements. The exact process and document naming can differ by local authority. Verify the specific project’s sanctioned plans, completion and occupancy status rather than relying on marketing statements.

What is an encumbrance certificate?

An encumbrance certificate is commonly used to review registered transactions or encumbrances recorded for a property over a specified period. It is useful but is not a complete substitute for a lawyer’s title search, because every risk or unregistered claim may not appear in one certificate.

Why is legal title verification important before buying property?

Property ownership can involve prior sale deeds, inheritance, mortgages, development agreements, powers of attorney, court matters, acquisition issues and approval conditions. A qualified property lawyer can trace the title chain and review documents instead of relying only on copies supplied by a seller or broker.

Agreement for sale and sale deed: what is the difference?

An agreement for sale records agreed terms and obligations for a future transfer, while a registered sale deed is the conveyance instrument that transfers ownership when executed and registered according to law. The exact legal effect depends on the transaction and jurisdiction, so use qualified legal advice for your purchase.

Ready-to-move or under-construction property: which is better?

The better choice depends on budget, financing, timeline, price, project risk and your need to inspect the finished product. Ready homes allow more direct inspection, while under-construction purchases may offer different payment schedules but require closer review of approvals, RERA disclosures, progress and completion timelines.

What should I check when buying a resale apartment?

Review seller title, prior sale deeds, loan or mortgage closure, society/association dues, property-tax status, sanctioned/actual use, parking and undivided share details where applicable, occupancy/completion records, maintenance history and physical condition. Use independent legal and technical review.

What should I check during a property site visit?

Check access and approach roads, surroundings, noise, drainage, water and power situation, daylight/ventilation, actual unit orientation, construction condition, common areas, parking, lifts, emergency access and the difference between what is shown on plans and what physically exists.

How do I compare property prices in two locations?

Compare like-for-like properties using carpet area, age, floor, orientation, parking, amenities, maintenance, possession status, title/approval quality and actual transaction evidence where available. A quoted price per square foot can be misleading when the area basis differs.

Is the lowest price per square foot always the best property deal?

No. The price basis may use different area definitions, and lower pricing can reflect location, age, legal status, construction quality, floor, access, maintenance or project risk. Compare total acquisition cost and property quality, not one headline rate.

What is the importance of sanctioned plans and approved layouts?

Approved plans and layouts show what the competent authority has permitted for the project or development. Buyers should compare the property being offered with the relevant approvals and verify material deviations or later revisions through appropriate professional and authority checks.

Should I verify a real-estate agent or broker?

Yes. Verify identity, business details and, where the law requires it, RERA agent registration. Also confirm who the agent represents, the brokerage amount, when it becomes payable and whether important statements about approvals, price or possession are documented by the promoter or seller.

What costs should I budget for besides the property price?

Depending on the transaction, additional costs can include stamp duty and registration, taxes, loan charges, legal/technical review, maintenance deposits, utility or association charges, brokerage, interiors and moving costs. Rates and tax treatment can change, so confirm current amounts with the relevant authority and professionals before purchase.

What is a property snag inspection before possession?

A snag inspection records visible defects and incomplete items before handover, such as door/window operation, tile or finish damage, plumbing leaks, electrical points, drainage slopes, cabinetry, paint and common-area issues that are within the inspection scope. Structural or concealed-system concerns require appropriate specialists.

What is undivided share of land in an apartment?

Undivided share, often called UDS, represents a proportionate interest in the land associated with an apartment ownership structure. The exact legal treatment and calculation depend on project documents and local law. Verify the sale deed, approved project documents and legal opinion.

Is bank loan approval proof that a property is legally safe?

No. A lender’s technical or legal review is performed for its own lending decision and should not replace the buyer’s independent legal due diligence. A buyer should still verify title, approvals, encumbrances, project documents and the actual property through qualified professionals.

What is mutation of property records?

Mutation generally updates ownership or revenue/municipal records after a transfer. It is an important administrative step but does not by itself replace a valid registered conveyance or cure title defects. Procedures vary by jurisdiction.

Why should property-tax receipts be checked before buying?

Tax records can help identify the property account and whether dues are outstanding, but payment of tax alone does not prove clear ownership. Review tax status together with title deeds, approvals, encumbrances and other transaction documents.

What should I verify before buying an open plot?

Check title and title chain, survey boundaries, access, land-use or conversion status where applicable, layout approval, encumbrances, road reservations, acquisition issues, utilities and physical possession. Use an independent lawyer and surveyor rather than relying only on plotted-layout marketing material.

Approved layout and unapproved layout: what is the difference?

An approved layout has been sanctioned by the competent planning authority subject to applicable conditions, while an unapproved subdivision may not have that authorization. Buyers should verify the approval number, plan and current status directly with the relevant authority.

What is a possession letter?

A possession letter generally records the promoter or seller offering or handing over possession under the transaction documents. It should be read together with the sale deed/agreement, completion or occupancy documentation where applicable, dues statement and physical handover checklist.

Can a builder change the floor plan after booking?

Material changes to plans, specifications or common areas can be regulated by the agreement, RERA framework and applicable approvals. Buyers should review the registered project disclosures, agreement terms and documented consent/approval requirements rather than relying on verbal explanations.

What should I check about parking before buying an apartment?

Confirm whether parking is allotted, its type and location, dimensions/access, what the agreement and sanctioned plan state, and whether any separate charge or ownership claim is legally permissible. Parking treatment can vary by project and law, so check documents carefully.

How should I compare maintenance charges between projects?

Compare what the charge actually includes: security, housekeeping, lifts, power backup, water systems, amenities, landscaping, sinking/reserve funds and taxes. A lower monthly figure can be misleading if major services are billed separately.

What documents should I collect at apartment possession?

Depending on the project, collect registered conveyance documents, possession/handover records, payment and no-dues statements, warranties/manuals, parking/allotment documents, approved or as-built information where provided, and relevant completion/occupancy records. Keep digital and physical copies.

What is a power of attorney in a property transaction?

A power of attorney authorizes another person to act for the principal within stated powers. Because validity, scope, revocation and registration requirements matter greatly in property deals, have the specific document reviewed by a qualified lawyer before relying on it.

What should I check when buying land converted from agricultural use?

Verify whether the intended residential or commercial use is legally permitted, whether required conversion or land-use approvals exist, title and access are clear, and the plotted development itself has any necessary layout approvals. Requirements vary by state and local authority.

How do I calculate rental yield on a property?

A simple gross rental yield compares annual rent with purchase price, but a more useful net view also considers vacancy, maintenance, property tax, brokerage, repairs and financing costs. Yield should be compared alongside location quality, liquidity and price risk.

Villa or apartment: which is better to buy?

The choice depends on land component, privacy, maintenance responsibility, amenities, security, location, budget, future expansion needs and resale market. Compare total ownership cost and legal/project quality rather than assuming one property type is always better.

Should I get an independent home inspection before buying a resale property?

An independent inspection can help identify visible defects in finishes, plumbing, electrical systems, moisture and maintenance condition. It does not replace structural investigation or legal due diligence, but it can improve negotiation and repair planning.

What is the difference between booking amount and token advance?

Both terms are used commercially for early payments, but their legal effect depends on the receipt, application form or agreement. Before paying, get written terms covering the property, amount, adjustment against price, cancellation, refund and conditions.
