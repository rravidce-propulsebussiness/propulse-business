Concrete defects & structural safety10 questions+
  1. Is honeycombing in concrete dangerous?
    Published answer

    Honeycombing is a voided or stony area where mortar has not properly filled around the coarse aggregate. It can be superficial, but deep honeycombing may reduce effective concrete section, expose reinforcement and allow water and chlorides to reach the steel. Do not decide safety from appearance alone. Photograph the area, record its location and extent, avoid covering it with plaster, and ask the structural engineer to inspect, sound and assess the concrete. Where needed, the engineer may specify cover removal, reinforcement checks, testing and an engineered repair. Active cracking, exposed or corroded bars, or honeycombing at a column joint or beam-column zone needs prompt assessment.

  2. What causes honeycombing in concrete columns?
    Published answer

    Common causes include inadequate vibration or compaction, congested reinforcement that blocks concrete flow, a mix that is too harsh or poorly proportioned, excessive free fall or segregation, leaking formwork joints, and placing concrete in lifts without proper consolidation. A column cage with closely spaced bars, laps or embedded services is particularly difficult to fill. Prevention starts before pouring: check bar spacing and cover, seal the shuttering, confirm the approved mix and workability, plan the pour sequence and access for the vibrator, and use trained workers. Adding water at site to make concrete flow is not a safe substitute for a properly designed workable mix.

  3. How do you repair honeycomb in an RCC column?
    Published answer

    There is no safe one-method repair for every honeycombed column. First have the structural engineer map the defect, determine its depth and whether reinforcement is exposed, loose or corroded, and assess whether the member has lost section or bond. The repair specification may require removal of unsound concrete to a sound substrate, cleaning and treatment of reinforcement, reinstatement with an approved repair mortar or micro-concrete, and curing. Deep defects may need designed form-and-pour repair or strengthening. Do not simply fill the surface with plaster, cement slurry or putty. Keep records and obtain the engineer’s inspection and acceptance before concealing the repair.

  4. Can a honeycombed column be repaired?
    Published answer

    Many honeycombed columns can be repaired, but repairability depends on defect depth, affected area, reinforcement condition, load stage and the column’s structural demand. A shallow surface void may need a different treatment from a deep void that exposes bars or interrupts the concrete core. Stop finishing work over the defect and request an on-site assessment by the structural designer. The engineer may call for measurements, sounding, cover checks or appropriate non-destructive/destructive tests before choosing a repair or strengthening design. Do not load or alter the member based on a contractor’s visual assurance alone.

  5. Are cracks in a newly constructed house normal?
    Published answer

    Some fine cracks can occur as plaster, mortar or concrete dries and shrinks, but a new house should not be assumed safe or defective from the word “crack” alone. Note the crack’s width, length, direction, location and whether it changes over time; photograph it with a scale and date. Hairline plaster cracks differ from diagonal cracks through masonry, cracks in beams or columns, widening cracks, displacement, water ingress or doors suddenly jamming. Report defects to the builder in writing and ask the structural engineer to assess any structural-looking or progressive crack. Do not hide cracks with putty before the cause is understood.

  6. Which wall cracks are dangerous in a house?
    Published answer

    Warning signs include cracks that keep widening, pass through masonry rather than only paint or plaster, run diagonally from door or window corners, appear with wall bulging or separation, occur alongside sagging floors or beams, or are accompanied by settlement and water damage. A single crack width does not reliably classify safety: material, location, movement and structural system matter. Photograph and monitor the crack without delaying professional review if it is growing or associated with distortion. Keep people away from visibly unstable areas and have a qualified structural engineer inspect before carrying out cosmetic repairs.

  7. Why are diagonal cracks appearing near doors and windows?
    Published answer

    Door and window corners are stress-concentration points, so diagonal cracks can arise from shrinkage, poor masonry bonding, inadequate or poorly supported lintels, differential movement, settlement or detailing problems where masonry meets an RCC frame. Check whether the crack is limited to plaster or continues through the wall, whether it appears on both sides, and whether it is widening. Photograph it and review the lintel, bearing, wall junction and foundation movement with the project engineer. Do not assume that adding mesh or filler is enough until the cause is identified.

  8. What is the minimum concrete cover for RCC columns and slabs?
    Published answer

    There is no single cover value that homeowners should apply to every column or slab. Required nominal cover depends on the structural member, exposure condition, fire-resistance requirement, concrete and reinforcement details, and the applicable design standard. The approved structural drawings should state the specified cover; verify it before concrete is poured using suitable cover blocks or spacers and an inspection record. Do not reduce cover to make reinforcement fit, and do not use broken brick pieces as substitutes. If the drawing is silent or site conditions differ, ask the structural designer to issue a written clarification.

    Official referencesBIS – IS 456 reinforced concrete code ↗
  9. How long should a newly cast roof slab be cured?
    Published answer

    Curing duration must follow the concrete specification, cement type, weather and the engineer’s instructions; a fixed number copied from another project is not a substitute. The goal is to keep the concrete sufficiently moist and protect it from rapid drying, especially during Hyderabad’s hot and windy periods. Start curing as soon as the surface can be treated without damage, use ponding or another approved method, prevent water from escaping and keep a daily site record. Do not remove formwork or props just because the surface looks hard; removal and reshoring must follow the structural engineer’s sequence and strength requirements.

  10. How can I check concrete quality before slab casting?
    Published answer

    Before the pour, compare the latest structural drawing and bar-bending schedule with the installed reinforcement. Check bar diameter, spacing, laps, anchorage, cover blocks, chairs, beam-column joints, shuttering line and level, tight joints, props, openings, sleeves, electrical conduits and plumbing embeds. Confirm concrete grade, approved mix or supplier, delivery/testing plan, access, vibrator availability, pour sequence and curing arrangements. Ask the engineer or authorised supervisor to sign a pre-pour checklist and photograph concealed work. Once concrete is placed, many of these items cannot be checked without intrusive work.

Cement, steel & construction materials10 questions+
  1. Which cement is best for house construction in India?
    Published answer

    There is no universally best cement brand or type for every part of a house. The structural designer and project specification should select a conforming cement type based on the concrete or masonry application, exposure, strength development, heat and construction method. Compare the applicable BIS standard and valid product marking, manufacturer traceability, batch and packing date, intact bags and storage condition. Buy from an authorised supplier, keep bags dry and raised off the floor, and use older stock first. Do not select cement only by advertising or switch type mid-project without the engineer’s approval.

    Official referencesBIS – Know Your Standard ↗BIS – cement and concrete standards ↗
  2. Which is better for house construction: OPC or PPC cement?
    Published answer

    OPC and PPC are both used in construction, but they behave differently and are not interchangeable in every specification. OPC is often selected where earlier strength development or a specified construction sequence is important; PPC contains pozzolanic material and can offer durability and lower heat benefits when properly specified and cured, while its early strength development may differ. The right choice depends on the structural mix design, exposure, placing schedule and curing plan. Follow the engineer’s specification and the cement’s applicable BIS standard rather than choosing by a blanket “stronger” claim.

    Official referencesBIS – Know Your Standard ↗BIS – cement and concrete standards ↗
  3. Which TMT steel is best for house construction?
    Published answer

    Choose reinforcement steel by the grade and ductility required in the structural drawings, not by brand popularity alone. Check the manufacturer’s identification rolled on the bar, invoice and batch traceability, applicable BIS licence/marking, test certificate and consistency of bar diameter and condition. Ask the engineer to approve any proposed brand or grade substitution before purchase or installation. Do not accept badly rusted, oily, bent or untraceable bars without assessment. Steel grade, ductility, bend performance and detailing all matter; a higher number on the label is not automatically a safer choice.

  4. What is the difference between Fe 500 and Fe 500D steel?
    Published answer

    The grade designation describes specified mechanical properties; the “D” suffix in Fe 500D denotes higher ductility requirements than ordinary Fe 500 under the relevant reinforcement standard. Ductility is important because reinforcement should be capable of deformation before fracture, particularly under dynamic or seismic demands. The exact grade must still match the structural designer’s drawings and applicable standard. Check the current BIS specification and test certificate for the supplied batch. Do not assume that Fe 500D can automatically replace every grade or that grade alone compensates for poor detailing and workmanship.

  5. Is Fe 550 steel better than Fe 500D for a house?
    Published answer

    Not automatically. Fe 550 has a higher specified yield strength, but a higher strength grade does not by itself make a residential structure safer or more economical. Ductility, elongation, bend performance, seismic detailing, bar congestion, anchorage and the design assumptions all matter. A structural engineer may specify a particular grade for a reason; changing it can affect reinforcement quantities and detailing. Never substitute Fe 550 for Fe 500D—or the reverse—without written approval and confirmation that the design and code requirements remain satisfied.

  6. How do I identify original TMT steel?
    Published answer

    Inspect the raised or rolled identification on the bar for the manufacturer and grade markings, and match them with the supplier invoice, bundle tags and batch test certificate. Confirm the product’s applicable BIS licence and marking through BIS resources; do not rely only on a printed sticker or QR code supplied by a seller. Check diameter and surface condition at delivery, keep material segregated by grade and batch, and retain a sample or records if the engineer requires testing. If markings are missing, inconsistent or suspicious, quarantine the batch and ask the engineer and supplier to resolve it before use.

  7. Which is better: red bricks or AAC blocks?
    Published answer

    Neither is best for every wall. Red clay bricks and AAC blocks differ in weight, dimensions, thermal behaviour, sound performance, water absorption, fixing requirements and workmanship. AAC is lighter and can reduce dead load, but needs compatible mortar, careful handling, correct anchors and manufacturer-recommended finishing. Brickwork may suit local availability and familiar workmanship, but quality and dimensions vary. Compare tested product properties, wall thickness, structural loads, plaster system, service chasing and total installed cost. Confirm the selected masonry system with the architect and structural engineer before changing the drawings.

    Official referencesBIS – National Building Code of India ↗BIS – Know Your Standard ↗
  8. Is M-sand better than river sand for house construction?
    Published answer

    Manufactured sand can be a suitable alternative to natural river sand when it is produced, graded and tested to the applicable specification. The important checks are grading, fines and dust content, particle shape, cleanliness, source consistency and suitability for the intended concrete or mortar. Concrete sand and plastering sand may need different grading characteristics. Ask for test results and have the mix designed or approved for the actual material; do not judge by colour or price alone. A change in sand source can change water demand and workability, so inform the engineer before use.

    Official referencesBIS – IS 383 aggregate standard ↗BIS – Know Your Standard ↗
  9. Which aggregate size is used for roof slab concrete?
    Published answer

    Aggregate size is selected by the concrete mix designer with regard to slab thickness, clear spacing between reinforcement, cover, placing method and the member geometry. The largest nominal size must be small enough to pass through the reinforcement and compact without segregation or honeycombing. A generic “20 mm for every slab” rule can be unsuitable in congested or thin sections. Confirm the approved mix design and aggregate grading with the structural engineer or ready-mix supplier, and do not change aggregate size at site without approval.

    Official referencesBIS – IS 383 aggregate standard ↗BIS – IS 10262 concrete mix proportioning ↗
  10. How can I check cement quality before buying?
    Published answer

    Check that each bag is sealed, dry and undamaged, with a readable manufacturer name, cement type, applicable standard marking, batch/packing details and traceable invoice. Avoid bags that are lumpy, damp, torn or stored directly on a wet floor or exposed to rain. Confirm the supplier is authorised and verify product claims through BIS resources where applicable. Visual checks cannot establish cement strength or genuineness conclusively; if a batch is doubtful, hold it and arrange appropriate testing rather than using it in structural concrete.

  3. What is the difference between GHMC and HMDA approval?
    Published answer

    GHMC and HMDA have different roles; they are not interchangeable labels for the same approval. HMDA is the metropolitan planning and development authority, dealing with the wider metropolitan planning framework and matters such as master-plan land use and layouts within its jurisdiction. Check the current BuildNow workflow and confirm the competent authority for the property. An HMDA layout approval by itself is not the same thing as approval of your individual house plan.

Follow the approved method statement and concrete specification. Do not add water to make a stiff mix easier to place; raise workability concerns with the engineer and supplier before placement.

It can result from poor placement or compaction, leaking formwork, congested reinforcement or an unsuitable mix. Do not hide it with plaster or patch it without assessment. Photograph and record the location, expose only as directed, and ask the structural engineer to assess its depth, extent and effect on reinforcement or durability. The repair method should be specified for the actual defect and documented before finishing.

Formwork removal timing and any subsequent loading must follow the project specification and engineer's instructions, not simply the appearance of the concrete.
