Do you need a soil test before building a house?

Foundation design depends on site conditions, building loads and geotechnical information. A neighbour’s footing size is not a reliable substitute for investigation and structural design.

Learn why soil investigation matters before house construction, what information a structural engineer may need and why copying a neighbour’s foundation can be risky.

Start your requirement · Find professionals

Important

Foundation decisions are structural and site-specific. Use a qualified geotechnical/structural professional for investigation interpretation and foundation design.

01

Soil conditions can change over short distances

Fill, expansive soils, groundwater, rock depth and past site use can vary even within the same neighbourhood.

02

The investigation should match the project

The required scope depends on building size, loads, site history and local conditions. The structural/geotechnical team should decide the appropriate investigation.

03

Use results in foundation design

Bearing conditions, settlement risk and groundwater information should feed into foundation selection and structural calculations.

04

Do not use “safe bearing capacity” as the only decision

Foundation performance also involves settlement, variability, groundwater, excavation conditions and structural interaction.

05

Include site investigation in pre-construction planning

Ask who arranges investigation, who reviews the report and whether foundation changes are treated as a variation before construction starts.
